# First-semester examination preparation

Open **Past-paper preparation** in the sidebar. Choose a subject and sitting, then use the original PDF button to check the complete command and scope of each question. The map uses concise topic labels, not a verbatim transcription.

## Study in three passes

1. **Recall:** make a definition, distinction or short list for every syllabus topic. Answer closed-book, then check terminology and correct omissions. Avoid relying on recognition alone.
2. **Explain:** practise medium answers with headings, a mechanism or procedure, and a relevant example. For comparisons, use the same dimensions on both sides.
3. **Evaluate and apply:** practise extended answers that link an explanation to evidence, a worked illustration, limitations and a conclusion. Read the command carefully: describing, comparing, applying and critically evaluating require different emphasis.

## Use the actual assessment scale

The two linked sittings use 70 marks over three hours: choose six short items worth two marks each, two medium items worth eight each, and one alternative from each of three extended-answer pairs worth fourteen each. The page checks this selection before starting its mock timer. This format describes those papers; check current examination instructions for changes.

For a short answer, answer the precise request efficiently. For a medium answer, establish the concept, develop the explanation and illustrate it. For an extended answer, plan a coherent argument and connect critique to the question. No official word limits or paragraph-to-mark formula have been assumed.

## A theory-evaluation paragraph that earns its place

Use this structure: **criterion → reasoned judgement → evidence or counterexample → implication**.

- Generates research: identify a prediction or researchable question.
- Is falsifiable: identify what observation could challenge the claim.
- Organizes data: explain what findings the account connects and what it leaves unexplained.
- Guides action: show a practical decision it supports, while distinguishing advice from demonstrated effectiveness.
- Is internally consistent: check definitions, assumptions and the compatibility of claims.
- Is parsimonious: compare explanatory adequacy with the number of assumptions required.

Do not equate falsifiability with already being false, parsimony with brevity, or practical influence with scientific confirmation. A whole theory does not become proven because it meets a checklist.

## A flexible study session

- Choose one syllabus unit and recall its major concepts without notes.
- Read only what you could not explain accurately.
- Answer one short item and plan one longer response.
- Compare the draft with the relevant theory or method notes.
- Record the missing concept, correct it and revisit it in a later session.

Work across every unit before concentrating solely on familiar topics. During a mock, obey internal choices and reserve time to check that every part of the selected question has been answered.
